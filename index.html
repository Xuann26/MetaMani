READ MORE Abstract+

Long-horizon robot manipulation is often learned and executed as complete trajectories. This binds reusable operations to fixed task orders, requires new demonstrations when familiar operations are reordered, and allows local failures to disrupt subsequent execution. Existing task-level policies and planners often lack executor-independent boundaries at which completed progress can be preserved and failed operations can be retried with alternative policies. We introduce MetaMani, a framework that organizes learning and execution around reusable manipulation units. Each unit specifies a local goal, an initiation condition, and observable success and failure criteria, while independently trained policies determine how to realize the intended state change. MetaMani selects compatible policies based on reliability estimates, verifies outcomes at unit boundaries, and retries locally or switches policies without replaying completed work. This enables checkpoint reuse across reordered task compositions without joint training or additional task-level demonstrations. Across RoboCasa365, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world tasks, MetaMani improves long-horizon completion. With a fixed low-level checkpoint, its single-policy configuration increases successful real-robot trials from 31/120 to 85/120. Heterogeneous per-MU dispatch reaches 98 of 120 (81.7%). These results demonstrate the effectiveness of organizing long-horizon manipulation around reusable and verifiable operations.

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

MetaMani uses a fixed MU vocabulary, a calibrated capability registry, and visually verifiable outcomes. Consecutive units share the same physical environment; their dynamics are not assumed to be independent.
